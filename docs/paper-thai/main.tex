\begin{abstract}
Thai grapheme-to-phoneme conversion is bottlenecked by labeled data:
high-quality dictionaries cover $\sim$10K entries. The prevailing 2026
recipe for low-resource tasks is distillation from large language
models. We show this is unnecessary and unsafe for phonological tasks:
LLMs hallucinate tones and phonotactics just as they hallucinate haraqat
in diacritization. Instead, we augment with a \emph{deterministic}
rule-based phonemizer (epitran) over 50K unlabeled Thai Wikipedia
sentences and continue fine-tuning a Thai-pretrained umt5-small. PER
falls from 3.24\% to \textbf{2.32\%} on a fixed 1,219-sentence
Kaikki-derived test set---surpassing the public baseline
(B-K/umt5-thai-g2p-v2-0.5k, 6.37\% on the same set) by 2.7$\times$. We
report extensive negative results: curriculum learning, output-vote
ensembles, and from-scratch seq2seq/CTC architectures all fail, locating
the bottleneck precisely in labeled-data coverage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Thai script omits vowel and tone information that phonology requires; G2P
must supply it. Published Thai G2P models (B-K/umt5-thai-g2p) are trained
on dictionaries of $\sim$10K entries.

Contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic augmentation}: epitran over 50K Wikipedia
        sentences $\rightarrow$ PER 3.24\%$\to$\textbf{2.32\%}, no LLM,
        no cost, no hallucination.
  \item \textbf{LLM unsuitability documented}: general LLMs cannot
        reliably produce Thai phonology (tone hallucination), mirroring
        the haraqat hallucination known for diacritization.
  \item \textbf{Bottleneck localization} via ablations: from-scratch
        seq2seq collapses; CTC plateaus at $\sim$70\% PER; curriculum
        \emph{hurts} at the data-limited optimum; output-vote ensembles
        are flat.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
B-K/umt5-thai-g2p-v2-0.5k: public Thai G2P (our base). ByT5
\cite{byt5}: byte-level multilingual pretraining. 2026 LLM-distillation
practice (e.g.\ DeepSeek-V4 ``ten teachers'') motivates our negative
result.

\section{Method}
Continue fine-tuning B-K/umt5-thai-g2p-v2-0.5k (300M) for 10 epochs on
9.7K Kaikki Thai--IPA pairs, LR $5{\times}10^{-5}$, beam 4. Then a
second stage on the union with 50K epitran-labeled Thai Wikipedia
sentences (epitran \texttt{tha-Thai}; filtering: Thai-script ratio,
length bounds; 143,979 raw lines skipped, 50K kept).

\section{Why not LLMs?}
Phonological labeling demands exactly the property LLMs lack:
faithfulness to a system of rules. Thai's five phonemic tones and
complex grapheme-phoneme correspondence invite fluent-sounding but wrong
output---the same failure documented for Arabic haraqat. Deterministic
phonemizers (epitran, eSpeak) never hallucinate; their systematic errors
are learnable biases that a fine-tuned model corrects, as our 2.32\%
result demonstrates.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Language-specific pretraining over scale}: Thai-exposed
umt5-small (300M) beats from-scratch ByT5-small by 10 points;
deterministic labels at scale close the remaining gap.

\section{Limitations}
Kaikki-format test; epitran labels are systematically biased (no tone
marks) and the model must reconcile two label distributions. Sentence
count of the test set is modest (1,219).
